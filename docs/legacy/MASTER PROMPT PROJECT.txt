This directive establishes the architectural upgrade and simulation blueprint for **Kyber-6G**. By synthesizing Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC), Continuous Variable Quantum Key Distribution (CV-QKD), advanced queueing theory, hardware acceleration, and dynamic multi-agent routing, Kyber-6G delivers a provably secure, latency-optimized, and resilient communication framework for autonomous aerial platforms.

### 3.1 140 GHz Terahertz Channel & Atmospheric Loss Model

The propagation path loss $PL(f, d)$ at $f = 140\text{ GHz}$ over a distance $d$ includes free-space path loss and molecular absorption loss defined by the Beer-Lambert law:

$$PL(f, d) = \left( \frac{4 \pi f d}{c} \right)^2 \cdot \exp\left( K_{abs}(f) \cdot d \right)$$

where $c$ is the speed of light, and $K_{abs}(f)$ is the frequency-dependent molecular absorption coefficient calculated from the HITRAN database based on temperature $T = 296.15\text{ K}$, pressure $p = 1\text{ atm}$, and relative humidity $RH = 50\%$.

The coherence time $T_c$ of the channel under Doppler shift caused by UAV velocity $v = 120\text{ m/s}$ is modeled as:

$$f_d = \frac{v}{\lambda_{thz}} = \frac{120\text{ m/s}}{2.14 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}} \approx 56.07\text{ kHz}$$

$$T_c \approx \frac{9}{16 \pi f_d} \approx \frac{9}{16 \pi (56074.7)} \approx 17.80\text{ }\mu\text{s}$$

### 3.2 Gilbert-Elliott Markov Burst-Error Channel

To model the severe fading bursts induced by $T_c = 17.80\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ relative to packet transmission duration, the link is structured as a two-state discrete-time Markov chain $S \in \{G, B\}$ (Good and Bad).

The transition probability matrix $\mathbf{P}_{GE}$ is:

$$\mathbf{P}_{GE} = \begin{bmatrix} P_{GG} & P_{GB} \\ P_{BG} & P_{BB} \end{bmatrix} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 - P_{GB} & P_{GB} \\ P_{BG} & 1 - P_{BG} \end{bmatrix}$$

The stationary state probabilities $\pi_G$ and $\pi_B$ are given by:

$$\pi_G = \frac{P_{BG}}{P_{GB} + P_{BG}}, \quad \pi_B = \frac{P_{GB}}{P_{GB} + P_{BG}}$$

Let $p_G$ and $p_B$ denote the bit error rates in the Good and Bad states respectively ($p_G \ll 1, p_B \approx 0.5$). The average bit error rate $\bar{P}_e$ and correlation coefficient $\rho_{channel}$ are:

$$\bar{P}_e = \pi_G p_G + \pi_B p_B$$

$$\rho_{channel} = 1 - P_{GB} - P_{BG}$$

For a PQC payload fragmented into $N_{frag}$ frames of size $L_{frame}$ bits, the Packet Delivery Ratio ($PDR$) under the Queue-Based Channel (QBC) approximation minimizing Kullback-Leibler divergence $D_{KL}(P_{GE} \parallel P_{QBC})$ is formulated as:

$$PDR(N_{frag}) = \pi_G (1 - p_G)^{N_{frag} \cdot L_{frame}} + \pi_B (1 - p_B)^{N_{frag} \cdot L_{frame}}$$

### 3.3 M/G/1 Pollaczek-Khinchine Queueing Mechanism

Because PQC key generation, encapsulation, and verification introduce bimodal micro-burst service times at the 6G gNB, the queueing delay is strictly non-exponential. The arrival process follows a Poisson process with rate $\lambda_{arr}$, while service times $S_{pqc}$ follow a general distribution with mean $E[S_{pqc}]$ and variance $\text{Var}(S_{pqc})$.

The traffic intensity is $\rho_{q} = \lambda_{arr} E[S_{pqc}] < 1$. The expected queue waiting time $W_q$ is governed by the Pollaczek-Khinchine formula:

$$W_q = \frac{\lambda_{arr} E[S_{pqc}^2]}{2(1 - \rho_{q})} = \frac{\lambda_{arr} \left( \text{Var}(S_{pqc}) + (E[S_{pqc}])^2 \right)}{2(1 - \lambda_{arr} E[S_{pqc}])}$$

Accounting for 3GPP 6G Transmission Time Interval (TTI) quantization boundaries $\tau_{tti}$ ($\mu \in \{3,4,5\}$, e.g., $\tau_{tti} = 125\text{ }\mu\text{s}, 62.5\text{ }\mu\text{s}, 31.25\text{ }\mu\text{s}$), the total queueing latency $T_{queue}$ is:

$$T_{queue} = \left\lceil \frac{W_q + E[S_{pqc}]}{\tau_{tti}} \right\rceil \cdot \tau_{tti}$$

### 3.4 CV-QKD Holevo Bound & Photon Catalysis Model

For inter-UAV Free Space Optical (FSO) links, Continuous Variable Quantum Key Distribution (CV-QKD) uses Gaussian-modulated coherent states (GMCS). Under log-normal atmospheric turbulence with variance $\sigma_R^2$, the channel transmissivity $\mathrm{T}$ fluctuates. Applying single-photon catalysis to the state improves the secret key rate $K_{cvqkd}$ under the Holevo bound:

$$K_{cvqkd} = \xi \cdot I(A; B) - \chi(B; E)$$

where $\xi$ is reconciliation efficiency, $I(A; B)$ is mutual information between Alice and Bob, and $\chi(B; E)$ is the Holevo bound on Eve's information:

$$\chi(B; E) = \sum_{i=1}^2 G\left( \frac{\lambda_i - 1}{2} \right) - \sum_{i=3}^5 G\left( \frac{\lambda_i - 1}{2} \right)$$

where $G(x) = (x+1)\log_2(x+1) - x\log_2(x)$, and $\lambda_{1..5}$ are the symplectic eigenvalues of the covariance matrices describing the quantum state after photon catalysis.

### 4.1 Hardware-Software Co-Design for ML-KEM-1024 & ML-DSA-87

To support sub-millisecond execution, the crypto-engine integrates specialized hardware optimizations:

1. Partitioned Memory Bank: Hardware memory is partitioned into 3 SRAM blocks (RAM0: $512 \times 64\text{ bits}$, RAM1: $512 \times 64\text{ bits}$, RAM2: $256 \times 64\text{ bits}$). Intermediate values are dynamically destroyed via in-place overwriting to comply with FIPS 203 memory sanitization requirements.


2. KRED Modular Multiplier & Unification: Uses the KRED reduction algorithm ($q = 3329$ for ML-KEM, $q = 8380417$ for ML-DSA). By calculating $c / 2^m = d_1 - k \cdot d_0 \equiv -k \cdot 2^m / n \cdot c \equiv c / n \pmod q$, multiplication by $n^{-1}$ in INTT is eliminated, allowing unified CT/GS butterfly execution.


3. Hierleaved Folding & Twiddle Optimization: The $2k$ NTT processors are folded into two parallel processing elements ($BU_{even}, BU_{odd}$) operating on a 64-bit datapath using Radix-2 Multi-path Delay Commutator (R2MDC) units. Twiddle storage for INTT ($\zeta^{-i}$) and PWM ($\zeta^{2\text{BitRev7}(2k)+1}$) is computed on-the-fly from forward NTT tables using:



$$\zeta^{-i} = q - \zeta^{n-i}$$

$$\zeta^{2\text{BitRev7}(2k+1)+1} = q - \zeta^{2\text{BitRev7}(2k)+1}$$

reducing ROM footprint by $66.7\%$.
4. Side-Channel Protection via Masked SHA-3 & Randomness Dispatcher: First-order DOM masking protects the Keccak core against power-analysis attacks. A hardware Randomness Dispatcher featuring 25 parallel instances of 64-bit unrolled Trivium/Bivium stream ciphers generates $1600\text{ bits/cycle}$ of fresh randomness.

## SECTION V: SWARM INTELLIGENCE, ROUTING, & MOSAIC WARFARE OPERATIONS

### 5.1 Mosaic Warfare Functional Abstraction

Following Qi et al., the UAV swarm ($N_{swarm} = 24\text{ to }140\text{ nodes}$) dynamically instantiates functional "mosaic" roles across the 6G NTN overlay:

* **Reconnaissance Nodes ($N_{rec}$):** Equipped with optical/infrared sensors and sub-THz radars; execute search and target identification.
* **Interference/EW Nodes ($N_{int}$):** Generate active directional jamming to suppress adversary air-defense sensors.
* **Integrated Rec-Attack Nodes ($N_{ira}$):** Carry modular munitions and real-time processing cores for high-value targets.
* **Cooperative Control Nodes ($N_{cc}$):** Act as local swarm commanders/relays interfacing with gNB/satellite segments.


* **Attack Nodes ($N_{att}$):** Precision strike units guided by real-time visual and sub-THz tracking from $N_{rec}$ nodes.



Target clustering is performed via multi-agent $K$-means clustering, while real-time task allocation uses Distributed Model Predictive Control (DMPC) combined with Differential Evolution.

### 5.2 Routing Protocol Mechanics: DORA vs. ZRP vs. DSRP

Under high mobility ($120\text{ m/s}$), network topologies experience rapid link breaks. The **Dynamic Optimal Random Access (DORA)** protocol is integrated for high-density V2N/UAV access.

```
  DORA Operation Flow:
  +-------------------------------+
  |  1. Collect System Parameters  |
  +---------------+---------------+
                  |
                  v
  +---------------+---------------+
  |  2. Set Boundary (t = T)      |
  +---------------+---------------+
                  |
                  v
  +---------------+---------------+
  |  3. While (t > 1):            |
  |     - Fetch data (p from s)   |

  +---------------+---------------+

  +---------------+---------------+

  +---------------+---------------+

                  v
  +---------------+---------------+

```

* **DORA:** Uses a two-stage sequential decision process (Planning and Transmission). In the planning phase, entering vehicles/UAVs announce parameters to the Access Point/gNB. In the transmission phase, slot allocation follows a dynamic optimal policy minimizing collision probability without requiring heavy routing table exchanges.


* **ZRP (Zone Routing Protocol):** Hybrid scheme combining proactive Intra-zone Routing Protocol (IARP) within radius $R_{zone}$ and reactive Inter-zone Routing Protocol (IERP) outside. Highly sensitive to $R_{zone}$ tuning; rapidly degrades under $120\text{ m/s}$ mobility due to border-node flooding.


* **DSRP (Dynamic Source Routing Protocol):** Purely reactive scheme appending full route paths to packet headers. High mobility causes extreme header overhead and route discovery storms, leading to low PDR.

## SECTION VI: FORMAL SECURITY PROOFS & VERIFICATION FRAMEWORKS

### 6.1 SVO-PFS Formal Logic Proof for X-Wing Hybrid KEM

We extend Syverson-Van Oorschot (SVO) logic to prove Perfect Forward Secrecy (PFS) for the X-Wing Hybrid scheme.

**Axioms for Post-Quantum Hybrid Exchanging:**

* **SVO-PQ1 (Hybrid Combination):** $\frac{P \equiv K_1 \wedge P \equiv K_2}{P \equiv \text{HKDF}(K_1 \parallel K_2)}$
* **SVO-PQ2 (Ephemeral Freshness):** $\frac{P \equiv \#(r_{ecc}) \vee P \equiv \#(r_{pq})}{P \equiv \#(\text{HKDF}(S_{ecdh} \parallel S_{kyber}))}$
* **SVO-PFS (Forward Secrecy):** $\frac{P \equiv \#(K_{final}) \wedge \text{Compromised}(sk_{identity})}{P \not\equiv K_{final} \text{ (Adversary cannot derive } K_{final} \text{)}}$

**Proof Derivation:**

1. Drone ($D$) generates ephemeral $r_{ecc}$ and $r_{pq}$. Thus, $D \equiv \#(r_{ecc})$ and $D \equiv \#(r_{pq})$.


2. By SVO-PQ2, $D \equiv \#(K_{final})$ and $gNB \equiv \#(K_{final})$.


3. Assume an adversary $\mathcal{A}$ compromises long-term static keys $sk_{D, static}$ and $sk_{gNB, static}$ at time $t_{future} > t_{session}$.
4. $S_{ecdh} = \text{X25519}(sk_{ecc}, pk_{server\_ecc})$ relies solely on ephemeral keys. $\mathcal{A}$ cannot compute $S_{ecdh}$ without solving the CDH problem over Curve25519.
5. $S_{kyber} = \text{Decaps}(sk_{pq}, C_{pq})$ relies solely on ephemeral $sk_{pq}$. $\mathcal{A}$ cannot compute $S_{kyber}$ without solving the MLWE problem for ML-KEM-1024.


6. By SVO-PQ1 and SVO-PFS, $\mathcal{A} \not\equiv K_{final}$. The protocol satisfies Perfect Forward Secrecy ($\blacksquare$).



### 6.2 BAN Logic Formal Verification for MORNeS & Server-Aided Authentication

Using BAN Logic postulates:

* **Message Meaning:** $\frac{P \equiv P \stackrel{K}{\leftrightarrow} Q, \quad P \triangleleft \{X\}_K}{P \equiv Q \sim X}$
* **Nonce Verification:** $\frac{P \equiv \#(X), \quad P \equiv Q \sim X}{P \equiv Q \equiv X}$
* **Jurisdiction:** $\frac{P \equiv Q \Rightarrow X, \quad P \equiv Q \equiv X}{P \equiv X}$

We prove that in Server-Aided Verification (IBS-SAV) and MORNeS, adding explicit directional tags $T_{dir} \in \{\text{Req}, \text{Resp}\}$ and nonce translation prevents reflection attacks.

**Reflection Attack Defeat Proof:**
In standard MORNeS, adversary $\mathcal{I}$ reflects $A$'s challenge $\{N_A, \text{ID}_A, \text{ID}_B\}_{K_{AM}}$ back to $A$. Under our modified schema, messages are tagged as:

$$M_1 = \{\text{TAG}_{A\to B}, N_A, \text{ID}_A, \text{ID}_B\}_{K_{AM}}$$

When $\mathcal{I}$ reflects $M_1$ to $A$, $A$ attempts to parse $\text{TAG}_{B\to A}$. Since $\text{TAG}_{A\to B} \neq \text{TAG}_{B\to A}$, $A$ rejects the message immediately at the BAN Message-Meaning phase, preventing challenge reflection ($\blacksquare$).

### 6.3 EUF-CMA Game-Hopping Proof for EMULSION

We construct a sequence of games $\text{Game}_0, \text{Game}_1, \text{Game}_2, \text{Game}_3$ to prove unforgeability for EMULSION SIB broadcasts:

* **$\text{Game}_0$:** Original EMULSION protocol execution with MAYO signatures.
* **$\text{Game}_1$:** Challenger aborts if a collision occurs on the HMAC-SHA-256 key chain. Adv Difference: $\le \frac{q_{hash}^2}{2^{256}}$.
* **$\text{Game}_2$:** Challenger replaces the TESLA-style HMAC verification with a random oracle. Adv Difference: Negligible under PRF security of HMAC-SHA-256.
* **$\text{Game}_3$:** Adversary attempts to forge the anchoring MAYO signature on the epoch header. Adv Difference: Bounded by $\text{Adv}_{\text{MAYO}}^{\text{EUF-CMA}}(t)$.

$$\text{Adv}_{\text{EMULSION}}^{\text{EUF-CMA}}(\mathcal{A}) \le \frac{q_{hash}^2}{2^{256}} + \text{Adv}_{\text{HMAC}}^{\text{PRF}}(\mathcal{A}) + \text{Adv}_{\text{MAYO}}^{\text{EUF-CMA}}(\mathcal{A})$$

Since MAYO is secure under the hardness of solving multivariate quadratic equations ($MQ$ problem), $\text{Adv}_{\text{EMULSION}}^{\text{EUF-CMA}}(\mathcal{A})$ is negligible ($\blacksquare$).

### Algorithm 1: X-Wing Hybrid KEM RRC Setup Handshake

* **Input:** Client Drone $D$, 6G Base Station $gNB$, System Parameters.
* **Output:** Established $K_{final}$, AES-256-GCM Secure Channel.

```python
def xwing_rrc_setup_handshake(drone_id, gnb_id):
    # Step 1: Drone Key Generation
    sk_ecc_d, pk_ecc_d = x25519_keygen()
    sk_pq_d, pk_pq_d = ml_kem_1024_keygen()
    
    # Step 2: RRC Setup Request (Uplink)
    rrc_request = serialize(pk_ecc_d, pk_pq_d, drone_id)
    send_over_thz_channel(rrc_request, gnb_id)
    
    # Step 3: gNB Processing
    sk_ecc_g, pk_ecc_g = x25519_keygen()
    s_ecdh_g = x25519_shared(sk_ecc_g, pk_ecc_d)
    c_pq, s_kyber_g = ml_kem_1024_encaps(pk_pq_d)
    k_final_g = hkdf_sha256(s_ecdh_g + s_kyber_g)
    
    # Step 4: RRC Setup Response (Downlink)
    rrc_response = serialize(c_pq, pk_ecc_g, gnb_id)
    send_over_thz_channel(rrc_response, drone_id)
    
    # Step 5: Drone Session Key Derivation
    s_kyber_d = ml_kem_1024_decaps(sk_pq_d, c_pq)
    s_ecdh_d = x25519_shared(sk_ecc_d, pk_ecc_g)
    k_final_d = hkdf_sha256(s_ecdh_d + s_kyber_d)
    
    assert k_final_d == k_final_g
    return k_final_d

```

* **Complexity:** Time: $\mathcal{O}(n \log n + \log p)$. Space: $\mathcal{O}(k^2)$ bytes memory overhead ($k=4$).

### Algorithm 2: Hardware-Accelerated Masked ML-KEM-1024 Processing

* **Input:** Polynomials $f, g \in R_q$, Masking Randomness Seed $R_{seed}$, $q=3329$.
* **Output:** Product Polynomial $h = f \cdot g \pmod{x^{256}+1}$.

```python
def hardware_accelerated_masked_poly_mult(f, g, r_seed):
    # Step 1: Randomness Generation via Unrolled Trivium (1600 bits/cycle)
    fresh_rand = trivium_dispatcher_generate(r_seed, bits=1600)
    
    # Step 2: DOM Masking - Split into Shares
    f_sh0, f_sh1 = dom_mask_split(f, fresh_rand[0:800])
    g_sh0, g_sh1 = dom_mask_split(g, fresh_rand[800:1600])
    
    # Step 3: Interleaved Folding NTT (2 Processing Elements: Even/Odd)
    f_ntt_even = r2mdc_ntt_fold(f_sh0[0::2], f_sh1[0::2], mode='DIT')
    f_ntt_odd  = r2mdc_ntt_fold(f_sh0[1::2], f_sh1[1::2], mode='DIT')
    
    g_ntt_even = r2mdc_ntt_fold(g_sh0[0::2], g_sh1[0::2], mode='DIT')
    g_ntt_odd  = r2mdc_ntt_fold(g_sh0[1::2], g_sh1[1::2], mode='DIT')
    
    # Step 4: Pointwise Multiplication with KRED Reduction
    h_ntt_even = kred_pwm(f_ntt_even, g_ntt_even, q=3329)
    h_ntt_odd  = kred_pwm(f_ntt_odd,  g_ntt_odd,  q=3329)
    
    # Step 5: Inverse NTT without n^-1 Scaling
    h_sh0 = r2mdc_intt_fold(h_ntt_even, mode='DIF')
    h_sh1 = r2mdc_intt_fold(h_ntt_odd,  mode='DIF')
    
    # Step 6: Unmasking and Overwrite Sanitization
    h = dom_unmask_combine(h_sh0, h_sh1)
    sanitize_sram_bank(f_sh0, f_sh1, g_sh0, g_sh1)
    return h

```

* **Complexity:** Time: $\mathcal{O}(n \log n)$ across 128 clock cycles. Space: $\mathcal{O}(n)$ in partitioned SRAM (7.37 kB total).

### Algorithm 3: DORA (Dynamic Optimal Random Access) Protocol for High-Mobility UAV Swarms

```python
def dora_protocol_access_schedule(uav_list, t_max, sigma):
    access_schedule = {}
    for uav in uav_list:
        t = t_max
        s = 0
        # Phase 1: Planning Phase
        while t > 1:
            p_request = uav.get_traffic_state(s)
            uav.send_ap_request(p_request)
            s = s + sigma
            t = t - 1
            
        # Phase 2: Transmission Phase
        optimal_slot = compute_optimal_policy(uav.id, s, t_max)
        access_schedule[uav.id] = optimal_slot
        
    return access_schedule

```

### Algorithm 4: MGS-MRPMC Signal Detection for Massive MIMO

* **Input:** Received Vector $y \in \mathbb{R}^{2N_r}$, Channel Matrix $H \in \mathbb{R}^{2N_r \times 2N_t}$, Parallel Chains $K$.
* **Output:** Detected Transmit Symbol Vector $\hat{x}_{opt}$.

```python
def mgs_mrPMC_signal_detection(y, H, QAM_order, N_t):
    K = math.floor(QAM_order * math.sqrt(N_t))
    chains = generate_uncorrelated_markov_chains(K, threshold_T=4)
    candidate_solutions = []
    
    # Parallel Markov Chain Execution
    for k in range(K):
        x_k = initialize_random_vector(N_t)
        stalling_count = 0
        while stalling_count < max_stalling_limit:
            for symbol_idx in chains[k]:
                # Probabilistic Choice: Gibbs Sampling vs Uniform Random
                if random.uniform(0, 1) < q_mix:
                    x_k[symbol_idx] = sample_uniform(QAM_order)
                else:
                    x_k[symbol_idx] = sample_gibbs_conditional(y, H, x_k, symbol_idx)
            stalling_count += 1
        candidate_solutions.append((compute_ml_cost(y, H, x_k), x_k))
        
    # Select Candidate with Minimum ML Cost
    best_cost, hat_x_opt = min(candidate_solutions, key=lambda item: item[0])
    return hat_x_opt

```

* **Complexity:** Time: $\mathcal{O}(K \cdot N_{iter} \cdot M^2)$ where $M = 2N_t$. Space: $\mathcal{O}(K \cdot M)$.

## SECTION X: DISCUSSIONS, LIMITATIONS, AND FUTURE DIRECTIONS

### 10.1 Identified System Bottlenecks

1. **TCP Head-of-Line (HoL) Blocking:** While hardware acceleration reduces ML-KEM decapsulation to $<50\text{ }\mu\text{s}$, frame fragmentation over sub-THz channels with $T_c = 17.8\text{ }\mu\text{s}$ causes burst losses. A single lost fragment stalls the TCP sliding window, triggering HoL blocking and RTO cascades.


2. **Dynamic Beam Tracking Overhead:** Maintaining narrow sub-THz pencil beams between UAVs moving at $120\text{ m/s}$ requires continuous beam alignment signaling, consuming up to $14\%$ of available MAC frame capacity.


3. **Masking Randomness Overhead:** Implementing first-order DOM masking on SHA-3/Keccak cores requires $1600\text{ bits/cycle}$ of fresh randomness. The Randomness Dispatcher (Trivium/Bivium ciphers) increases total hardware ASIC area by $300\%\text{--}500\%$, posing resource challenges for micro-UAV payloads.



### 10.2 Future Architectural Extensions

To overcome these limitations in Phase 3 of the project:

* **Stochastic Network Calculus (SNC):** Replace deterministic queueing bounds with SNC to establish probabilistic latency-violation bounds under heavy THz fading.
* **Application-Layer Application of Rateless Raptor Codes:** Implement fountain coding over fragmented PQC handshakes, allowing UAVs to recover key material from any arbitrary set of received symbols, eliminating TCP retransmissions and resolving HoL blocking completely.
